§ 01 / Foundation

Design Principles

Four principles guide every decision. They reflect how MacDonald-Miller approaches a job site: precise, useful, honest, and deeply human.

— 01

Built, Not Decorated

Every element earns its place. Structure is honest, materials are real, and ornament serves function. The grid is the steel frame — visible when it should be, invisible when it shouldn't.

— 02

Industrial With Warmth

Hard edges, monospaced metadata, and disciplined typography give the system its trade-built character. Warm paper backgrounds and human imagery keep it from feeling cold or corporate.

— 03

Red Means Something

The MM red is structural, not decorative. It marks the primary path, calls attention to the critical action, and signals brand authorship. Used sparingly, it has weight.

— 04

People in the Frame

Buildings are the backdrop — people are the subject. Imagery shows hands at work, faces with names, teams in motion. The product is mechanical; the value is human.

§ 10 / Voice

Voice & Tone

Confident, plain-spoken, and built on craft. We are tradespeople and engineers, not marketers. We respect the reader's time and intelligence — we don't oversell.

Do
  • Lead with people. "The team behind the team" beats "industry-leading solutions" every time.
  • Use direct, working-class English. Short sentences. Real verbs.
  • Earn claims with specifics — sixty years, 1,500 experts, 13 locations, 100,000 SF prefab shop.
  • Name the hard part. "Tight timelines. Aging infrastructure. Shifting codes." Then say how we lean in.
  • Sign off with action: a phone number, an address, an invitation.
Don't
  • Don't use "solutions," "leverage," "synergy," or any other corporate filler. Say what you mean.
  • Don't write hero copy without a verb in the first three words.
  • Don't use exclamation points. The work is the emphasis.
  • Don't hedge with "we strive to" or "we aim to." Either we do it or we don't.
  • Don't treat the trades as a brand prop. The hardhat is a real piece of gear.
